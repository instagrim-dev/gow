% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
When repeated work on a hard problem stops producing information, we propose
treating the work itself as data. \emph{Geometry of Work} (GoW) represents a
history of problem-solving attempts as a structured search space---a
\emph{shape-space}---whose regions, boundaries, and conserved properties contain
information about how search is failing or progressing. GoW separates structural
shape from outcome and epistemic status, mines the geometry for boundaries
between outcome regimes, proposes a structural move $\Delta S$ that crosses a
boundary, and projects the move back into the original domain for verification.
We formalize the GoW loop
(\textsc{Map}$\to$\textsc{Boundary}$\to$\textsc{Move}$\to$\textsc{Project}$\to$
\textsc{Measure}), describe \texttt{newf}---an instrumented realization with
typed representations, immutable provenance, and an adversarial challenge
lifecycle---and report exploratory experiments on an Erd\H{o}s--Straus study
corpus organized as a four-experiment progression, followed by a fifth,
deterministically checked demonstration. In negative controls, the
system abstains rather than manufacturing structure when preconditions are
absent. In curated-feature trials, shape-guided context produced the only
proposal a blinded reviewer judged a full criterion recovery, though identifier
masking rather than full concealment of the experimental condition was
achieved. In discovery trials, an adjudication quorum classified 10 of 23
generated proposal occurrences as defensible-novel (i.e., not present in the
reference ledger), collapsing into five distinct themes; the predeclared aggregate effectiveness
endpoint was not met. One model-generated reference-absent hypothesis
(``density-averaging ceiling'') was subjected to a seven-part, model-assisted
analysis that proposed a boundary refinement splitting one structural ceiling
into two sub-mechanisms; counterexample coverage remained incomplete and the
mathematical arguments were not independently verified. Finally, in a
preregistered closed decision loop scored by an exact integer checker --- the
paper's only reproducible-computation evidence --- a recorded map revision
changed the committed next action from a failing local move to a succeeding
global one, and a matched-budget comparison measured the map's ordering value
(20/20 verified witnesses at 20 checker submissions vs.\ 3/20 at 57 for an
undirected arm, at a frozen budget of three submissions per instance) at
demonstration scale on one hard instance class. The
experiments do not
establish mathematical validity, general discovery effectiveness, or autonomous
theorem solving; they demonstrate the framework's epistemic behavior and a
constituent set of capabilities.
\end{abstract}

% ── 1. Introduction ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem with repeated hard work}

Some problems resist sustained effort not because they are poorly understood,
but because the effort itself is not producing new information. A research team
accumulates twelve serious attempts on a conjecture; each attempt is more
sophisticated than the last; none resolves the case. A software team iterates
on a design; each iteration adds complexity; the core problem remains. An
optimizer runs for hours; the objective barely moves.

In each case, the problem is not a lack of effort or ingenuity. The problem is
that \emph{the space of attempts is being searched linearly}, treating each new
attempt as independent of the last. The accumulated history of failure is
discarded as consumed effort rather than read as structured evidence.

This paper is about what to do instead.

\subsection{Geometry of Work}

The key insight is that a population of attempts on a problem --- including all
the failed ones --- has a geometry. Attempts cluster by the structure they
instantiate. Clusters partition by outcome. The partitions reveal boundaries.
Boundaries are the structural conditions that separate the work that has
progress potential from the work that does not. A new attempt can be designed
to deliberately cross a boundary rather than continue inside a known failure
regime.

We call this framework \emph{Geometry of Work} (GoW). The methodology separates
shape-space reasoning from domain-space verification: the geometry is
constructed and explored in shape-space (the space of structural descriptions),
and proposed crossings are projected back into domain-space for classical
evaluation. Shape-space search is inherently uncertain; domain-space evaluation
applies the strongest verifier available to the projected candidate. Its
authority is not intrinsic: a domain-space check is authoritative only about
what it checked, against which claim, under which assumptions --- an
incomplete or misdirected check confers nothing. Keeping the two spaces
separate, with the verifier's coverage recorded, is the epistemic core of the
framework.
\[
W
\xrightarrow{S}
\mathcal{G}(W)
\xrightarrow{B}
\Delta S
\xrightarrow{\Pi}
W'
\xrightarrow{O}
\mathcal{G}'(W \cup \{W'\})
\]

\subsection{Contributions}

We make four claims, deliberately ordered from broadest to narrowest:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
  \item \textbf{Conceptual.} A history of problem-solving work can itself be
    represented as a structured search space whose geometry contains useful
    information about how search is failing or progressing.
  \item \textbf{Methodological.} Conserved shapes, outcome regimes, boundaries,
    and structural deltas provide a disciplined way to choose informative next
    work rather than continuing linear search.
  \item \textbf{Systems.} \texttt{newf} operationalizes this methodology using
    typed, provenance-carrying representations, epistemic state tracking,
    adversarial challenge, and domain projection.
  \item \textbf{Empirical (narrow).} In project-authored Erd\H{o}s--Straus study
    corpora, LLMs can propose structural regularities not explicitly supplied as
    reference properties, and shape-guided context produced the only proposal
    judged a full criterion recovery of a withheld structural move; the experiments do \emph{not}
    establish mathematical validity, general discovery effectiveness, or
    autonomous theorem solving.
\end{enumerate}

% ── 2. Motivating Example ────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Motivating Example}
\label{sec:example}

Consider a research team that has spent several years attempting to prove the
Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture ($4/n = 1/a + 1/b + 1/c$ has a solution for all
$n \geq 2$)~\cite{elsholtz2024erdos} without success. They have accumulated roughly a dozen serious
approaches: algebraic identities, modular arithmetic constructions, sieve
methods, class-number arguments, geometric interpretations, and analytic
estimates. None has resolved the general case.

\paragraph{Conventional search (the failure mode).}
In the conventional model, the team's next step is to \emph{think of something
new}. ``New'' here means surface novelty: a different proof idea, a different
representation, a different tool. The history of attempts is consumed context,
not data. Each new attempt is a fresh start. After twelve years, the team has
twelve attempts and no useful accumulated knowledge about the structure of
the problem's resistance.

\paragraph{GoW (treating work as data).}
Under GoW, the team first \emph{maps} the twelve attempts. Each is given a
structural description: what objects it reasons over, what it assumes, what
operations it performs, what properties it preserves. The descriptions are
normalized to a common vocabulary. Clustering reveals four mechanistic families,
here treated for simplicity as coextensive with four regimes (in general a
single mechanistic family can contain more than one outcome regime).
Three are failure-conditioned; one contains the two partial results in the corpus.

Mining the failure families produces a candidate shape claim: every approach
that has stalled so far has an \emph{averaging ceiling} in its rate argument.
The polylogarithmic or slower growth of its key aggregate is insufficient to
force a solution to exist. The claim is proposed, challenged, and produces
a boundary delta: there are two sub-mechanisms (rate-ceiling and reach-ceiling)
that were previously conflated. Coverage of the challenge is left incomplete
in this stylized telling, matching the real artifact's actual disposition
(below).

The boundary delta is now a structural target. The team asks: \emph{what approach
would cross this boundary?} The answer --- a construction that avoids polylog
rate arguments entirely, using instead a sparse algebraic identity that works
modulo a structured residue class --- is a proposal in shape-space. Translating
it back into domain-space produces a concrete construction to investigate.

\paragraph{The difference.}
Conventional search produces another attempt. GoW produces an attempt whose
structural rationale is legible: it targets a specific, challenged
boundary in the failure geometry. If the attempt fails, the landing point is
re-ingested and the geometry is updated. Failure is still failure; but now it
is a measurement.

This example is a \emph{stylized composite}, not a report of an executed run.
Its elements correspond to real artifacts at substantially reduced scope: in
Pilot-004 a density/averaging-ceiling hypothesis (N2a) was proposed for
\emph{two} corpus approaches (es-05, es-10) --- not for every stalled attempt
--- and a seven-part challenge produced a proposed refinement into rate-ceiling
vs.\ reach-ceiling sub-mechanisms, with two of the seven probes not fully
discharged (\cref{sec:results-challenge}); in Pilot-003 a fiberwise
lattice-point proposal (B3 rank 4) was judged a full criterion recovery by a
blinded model reviewer, though identifier masking rather than full concealment
of the experimental condition was achieved
(\cref{sec:results-pilot003}). The four-family
clustering, the ceiling shared by \emph{all} stalled approaches, the claim
that the challenge \emph{survives} the boundary delta, and the
specific boundary-crossing construction above are illustrative inventions and
appear in no experiment record; the real N2a artifact's disposition is
refinement proposed with coverage incomplete, not survival.

% ── 3. Semantic Model ────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Semantic Model: The Geometry of Work}
\label{sec:semantic}

\begin{definition}[Work item]
A \emph{work item} is a triple $W_i = (S_i, O_i, E_i)$ where $S_i$ is a
structural description (\emph{shape}), $O_i \in \mathcal{O}$ is an observed
outcome, and $E_i$ is the evidence supporting the description and outcome.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural shape]
A \emph{structural shape} $S$ is an outcome-blind description of a piece of
work: its representation, assumptions, operators, and preserved properties.
Two attempts with the same shape are the same point in shape-space even if
written in different notation; two attempts with different shapes are different
points even if they use the same words.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Geometry of Work]
Given a population of work items $\{W_1, \ldots, W_n\}$, the \emph{Geometry
of Work} is the tuple
\[
\mathcal{G}(W) = (R, B, H, V, T)
\]
where:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $R$: regimes---regions of shape-space associated with an outcome
    distribution;
  \item $B$: boundaries---structural conditions separating regimes;
  \item $H$: shape claims---conserved or discriminating structural properties;
  \item $V$: voids---unoccupied or under-sampled structural combinations;
  \item $T$: trajectories---paths through shape-space traced by successive work.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Regime]
A \emph{regime} $r \in R$ is a region of shape-space associated with an
outcome distribution. Formally, it is a set of work items whose structural
shapes cluster together under a similarity criterion, together with the
conditional outcome distribution $P(O \mid \text{shape} \in r)$. Two common
regime types are \emph{failure-conditioned} (work that did not progress the
problem) and \emph{success-conditioned} (work that made progress). Partial
successes define intermediate regimes. Regimes are empirically estimated, not
axiomatically given.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Boundary]
A \emph{boundary} $b \in B$ is a structural condition that separates two
regimes with distinct outcome distributions. A boundary has the form
\[
b = \bigl(\text{property } p,\; r_1,\; r_2 \bigr)
\]
where $p$ is absent in $r_1$ and present in $r_2$ (or vice versa), and the
difference in outcome distributions across $p$ is greater than the noise in
the estimates. A boundary is not a sharp line but a structural discriminant:
the evidence establishes that $p$ changes across the boundary, not that
the boundary is a hyperplane.

A \emph{boundary delta} $\delta(b)$ is the minimal structural condition
separating a counterexample (or observed split) from a claim's support
population. It arises during adversarial challenge and is itself evidence:
a sharp delta is a \emph{resolution gain}, not a failure, because it reveals
a tighter boundary.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural delta]
A \emph{structural delta} $\Delta S$ is a proposed change in structural shape
that, if instantiated, would cross a boundary from one regime toward another.
It is an action in shape-space, not an assertion about domain-space. It must
be stated as a concrete difference in at least one structural field (representation,
assumptions, operators, preserved properties) and must argue that the change
is mechanistically distinct from known failure families, not merely notational.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Projection and landing point]
A \emph{projection} $\Pi_{\text{domain}} : \Delta S \to W'$ translates a
structural delta into a domain-native candidate that a classical verifier can
evaluate. The translation is not unique, and the projection step is inherently
model-assisted. The resulting $W'$ is a \emph{proposed work item}, not yet
a member of the observed geometry.

A \emph{landing point} is the observed result of evaluating $W'$ in the
domain:
\[
\Lambda(W') = \bigl( O(W'),\; E(W'),\; \text{strength} \bigr)
\]
where $\text{strength}$ records the tier of the verifier that established $O(W')$
(formal proof, reproducible computation, model judgment, etc.). The landing
point, together with the shape $S(W')$, is then inserted back into the geometry,
updating $R$, $B$, $H$, and $T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Shape-space and domain-space are distinct]
A structural delta $\Delta S$ proposes a crossing in shape-space. It does not
establish a domain result. Projection $\Pi_{\text{domain}}(\Delta S) = W'$
produces a candidate; verification of $W'$ produces a landing point. These three
events are separate, carry separate epistemic weights, and must not be conflated.
Proposing a crossing is not the same as landing across.
\end{remark}

Each component of the geometry encodes a different kind of information:
$R$ reports where work has been; $B$ reports what structurally separates
outcome classes; $H$ records the conserved shapes that make boundaries
predictive; $V$ records the under-sampled or unoccupied structural combinations
(voids); and $T$ records how successive work has moved through shape-space.
A practitioner with access to only $B$ and $H$ has enough to propose $\Delta S$
and begin a projection; the rest of the geometry improves prior estimates and
informs search policy.

% ── 4. Epistemic Model ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Epistemic Model: Claims, Evidence, and Promotion}
\label{sec:epistemic}

Searching in shape-space is fluent precisely because it is not being certified
as it happens. That fluency is also dangerous: a coherent structural argument
\emph{feels} like knowledge. The epistemic model exists to ensure that nothing
is \emph{counted} as knowledge until something other than fluency has confirmed it.

\subsection{A claim is a tuple, not a point on a ladder}

\begin{definition}[Shape claim]
A \emph{shape claim} is a tuple
\[
\text{ShapeClaim} = (\text{shape},\; \text{conditioning},\; \text{role},\;
\text{epistemic state})
\]
where the four components are orthogonal axes, not a single confidence scalar.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{shape} --- the conserved structure being asserted; does not
    change when confidence changes or scope narrows.
  \item \textbf{conditioning} --- the outcome-population the shape was observed
    over: \emph{failure-conditioned}, \emph{success-conditioned}, \emph{mixed},
    or \emph{frontier-conditioned} (observed over generated-but-not-yet-evaluated
    proposals; not exercised by any experiment in this paper).
  \item \textbf{role} --- the interpretive standing: regularity, obstruction,
    enabling condition, or boundary hypothesis (see below).
  \item \textbf{epistemic state} --- how much adversarial pressure the claim
    has survived: \emph{proposed}, \emph{challenged}, \emph{surviving},
    \emph{weakened}, \emph{split}, \emph{falsified}, or \emph{operator-attested}.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\subsection{The four separations}

Four pairs of concepts must never be collapsed. Each conflation is a failure
mode.

\paragraph{1. Shape $\neq$ conditioning.}
A structural shape is outcome-blind. Attaching a population is a separate,
explicit act that changes the meaning of the claim. The same shape observed
over failures vs.\ successes is a different belief: the first is a hypothesis
about what correlates with failure; the second is a hypothesis about what
correlates with progress.

\paragraph{2. Observed regularity $\neq$ obstruction.}
That a property recurs across the failure regime is a \emph{descriptive} fact.
That the property \emph{prevents} progress is an \emph{explanatory} claim.
The claim role axis exists precisely to prevent regularity from masquerading as
obstruction. Roles:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \emph{regularity} --- shape conserved across the sampled population;
    no causal reading claimed.
  \item \emph{obstruction} --- regularity + discrimination against successes +
    challenge survival; the shape \emph{may} causally block progress.
  \item \emph{enabling condition} --- regularity conserved across successes;
    the shape may be necessary for progress.
  \item \emph{boundary hypothesis} --- the minimal structural difference between
    a failure regime and a success regime; the same object as the structural
    delta $\Delta S$ defined in \cref{sec:semantic}, written here as
    $\Delta(F, S)$ to make the two regimes explicit, that search
    proposes to cross.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{3. Model judgment $\neq$ verification.}
A provider may propose any claim. Consensus among models is still model judgment.
Verification requires a mechanism stronger than model judgment: a
deterministic computation, a reproducible experiment, or an independent proof.
Criticism from an independent model family that did not generate the original
claim can strengthen or weaken confidence, but it remains model judgment
(tier~4 below); it does not verify. What any evidence contributes is described
by what was checked, against which claim, under which assumptions, and with
what unresolved coverage --- provider independence is one useful attribute of
that account, not a substitute for it.

\begin{definition}[Verification hierarchy]
Claims are strengthened through the following ordered tiers (higher tiers
supersede lower ones as evidence, but lower tiers are not discarded):
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=\arabic*.]
  \item Formal proof or deterministic check
  \item Reproducible computation or experiment
  \item Independently sourced evidence (not model-derived)
  \item Cross-model or independent-critic agreement
  \item Single-model judgment
\end{enumerate}
No tier automatically invalidates lower ones, but the recorded evidence strength
must remain explicit. A surviving challenge at tier~5 is weaker than a surviving
challenge at tier~2; both are recorded.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{4. Absence $\neq$ negation.}
An unobserved property in a structural description is not a falsified one.
An absent field means ``not observed''; a falsified claim means ``a
counterexample was found.'' Coercing absence to negation silently extends a
claim beyond its evidence base.

\subsection{Epistemic state transitions}

A shape claim enters the lifecycle at \emph{proposed} and may transition as
follows:
\[
\text{proposed}
\xrightarrow{\text{adversarial challenge}}
\begin{cases}
  \text{surviving} & \text{(no completed probe falsified; coverage recorded)} \\
  \text{weakened} & \text{(scope narrowed; boundary delta found)} \\
  \text{split} & \text{(claim decomposes into two non-trivially distinct children)} \\
  \text{falsified} & \text{(counterexample found and verified)}
\end{cases}
\]
\emph{Surviving} is a coverage claim, not a completeness claim: the
implemented contract requires at least one adversarial attack completed with
a negative result, while other probes may individually be inapplicable or
inconclusive on the given corpus. The per-probe record --- which attacks
completed, which could not be executed, and why --- travels with the status.
A \emph{surviving} claim may be independently verified,
which advances it toward \emph{operator-attested} or (with proof-level evidence)
toward an established result. A \emph{weakened} claim has no direct path to
attestation: it must first re-earn \emph{surviving} through a further
challenge over its narrowed scope. (In the implementation, \emph{split} is
likewise not a resting state: it is recorded as a weaken transition whose
child claims are persisted as new \emph{proposed} claims that must survive
their own challenges, inheriting no authority from the parent.) No transition
may occur automatically; each
requires explicit evidence and an operator or independent critic to record the
transition.

\subsection{The adversarial challenge}

A shape claim submitted for challenge is probed by seven operations:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=\textbf{C\arabic*.}]
  \item Find a known failed attempt that violates the claim.
  \item Construct a synthetic failed attempt that violates the claim.
  \item Find a success that still preserves the claim (contrast stability).
  \item Lower the abstraction level and test whether the claim splits.
  \item Raise the abstraction level and test whether two claims merge.
  \item Identify sampling or publication bias in the observed population.
  \item Distinguish correlation from causal obstruction.
\end{enumerate}

A \emph{weaken} or \emph{split} outcome is a \emph{resolution gain}: the
boundary delta $\delta$ sharpens the claim's scope rather than refuting it.
The weakened claim plus $\delta$ are both more informative than the original
claim. A sharp delta is thus preferable to a vacuous survive, and a principled
falsification is preferable to an unchallenged hypothesis.

\subsection{Anti-fractal discipline}

Recursive refinement is productive but must not fractal-expand indefinitely.
The governing rule: a boundary delta licenses at most \emph{one} grounded
child hypothesis, which enters the lifecycle at \emph{proposed} with no
inherited authority from the parent. Children are not admitted until two or
more independently motivated instances of the delta exist in the corpus.

\section{Shape-Guided Search}
\label{sec:method}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Shape-Guided Search: \textsc{Map $\to$ Boundary $\to$ Move $\to$
  Project $\to$ Measure}}
\label{alg:gow}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Population of prior work $W$; observations $O$; domain verifier $V$
\Ensure Updated geometry $\mathcal{G}'$ and (possibly) improved candidate $W'$
\Statex
\State \textsc{Map}: Infer structural descriptions $S(W)$; reconstruct
  $\mathcal{G}(W) = (R, B, H, V, T)$
\State \textsc{Regime}: Identify outcome-conditioned regions in $\mathcal{G}$
\State \textsc{Shape}: Propose conserved or discriminating structure $H'$
\State \textsc{Challenge}: Seek counterexamples, success contrasts,
  abstraction splits, redundancy, bias
\State \textsc{Boundary}: Identify minimal structural distinction separating
  relevant regimes
\State \textsc{Move}: Choose structural delta $\Delta S$ crossing the boundary
\State \textsc{Project}: Compile $\Delta S$ into domain-native candidate
  $W' = \Pi_{\text{domain}}(\Delta S)$
\State \textsc{Verify}: Evaluate $W'$ using domain verifier $V$; record
  landing point $(O(W'), \text{strength})$
\State \textsc{Update}: Insert $W'$, $S(W')$, $O(W')$ into $\mathcal{G}$;
  mutate search policy
\State \Return to Step~1 with updated $\mathcal{G}'$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Step 1 (Map).}
Normalize heterogeneous work into outcome-blind structural shapes; canonicalize
labels to stable, versioned vocabulary identifiers; fingerprint each shape
deterministically; group shapes into mechanistic families; partition families
by observed outcome into regimes. Report coverage and gaps. The output is an
explicit geometry: $n$ distinct families, their boundaries, and where the
sample is thin.

\paragraph{Steps 2--4 (Regime, Shape, Challenge).}
Mine the geometry for structure worth crossing. Shape claims are the most
developed instrument, but other readings are equally valid: clusters identify
regions; voids identify unoccupied structural combinations; gradients identify
directions along which outcome improves; bridges identify structure shared
between failure and success families; separatrices are the conserved properties
that most strongly discriminate outcome classes.

A shape claim that survives challenge --- or sharpens through weakening and
boundary-delta extraction --- is stronger evidence than an unchallenged
hypothesis.

\paragraph{Steps 5--6 (Boundary, Move).}
Identify the minimal structural condition separating the relevant regimes.
Choose a structural delta $\Delta S$ that crosses it. A proposal must be
mechanistically distinct from known failure families: it must change at least
one structural field in a non-cosmetic way and name its nearest failure family
and why it is still distinct. The conceptual objective is:
\[
\arg\max_{\Delta S}
\frac{\text{mechanistic distance} + \text{invariant violation} +
      \text{expected information gain}}{\text{evaluation cost} + \text{redundancy}}
\]
Fake precision in this score is undesirable; component-wise or ordinal
judgments are acceptable.

\paragraph{Step 7 (Project).}
Translate $\Delta S$ into a domain-native candidate $W'$ that a classical
verifier can evaluate. This step is inherently model-assisted and not unique.
The projection is a hypothesis about how a shape-space move compiles into
domain-space; it is not a proof.

\paragraph{Steps 8--9 (Verify, Update).}
Evaluate $W'$ with the strongest verifier available (see the verification
hierarchy, \cref{sec:epistemic}). Record the landing point. Whether $W'$
succeeds or fails, insert $\bigl(W', S(W'), O(W')\bigr)$ back into
$\mathcal{G}$ and update the search policy. A failed crossing is informative
if it narrows the search space, falsifies an invariant, identifies a tighter
boundary, or reveals structural redundancy. Failure invariants are not an
embarrassment; they are measurements of the terrain.

\subsection{Recursive refinement}

Challenge (step 4) is itself a recursive pass of the same loop. When challenge
produces a boundary delta $\delta(b)$, it opens a micro-loop:
\[
F_0
\xrightarrow{\text{compress}} I_0
\xrightarrow{\text{challenge}} (I_1, \delta_1)
\xrightarrow{\text{micro-loop}} \cdots
\]
where $F_0$ is the original failure population, $I_0$ the candidate claim,
$I_1$ the weakened claim, and $\delta_1$ the minimal condition separating the
support from the counterexample. Each delta narrows the map's resolution before
any crossing is attempted. The anti-fractal rule (\cref{sec:epistemic}) prevents
this recursion from expanding indefinitely.

% ── 6. System ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{System: \texttt{newf}}
\label{sec:system}

\texttt{newf} is an instrumented realization of the GoW loop. Its technical
contribution is not the methodology itself but the answer to a specific
engineering question:

\begin{quote}
How do you build software that lets fuzzy, model-native operations coexist
with deterministic evidence and immutable provenance?
\end{quote}

The answer is strict separation: model-native operations (compress, contrast,
abstract, propose) produce \emph{claims} and \emph{candidates} that enter the
system with an explicit epistemic status and never upgrade it silently. Every
state transition requires explicit evidence. Every artifact is content-addressed
and linked to its derivation. The system owns durable state; models execute
bounded inference operations inside it.

\subsection{Architecture}

The GoW loop is realized as a layered CLI pipeline (Go; SQLite-backed; offline
first). The layers are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
  \item \textbf{Work ingestion.} Each piece of work (a prior approach, a
    paper, a proof attempt) is ingested as an immutable record with source
    provenance. The ingestion step does not assign structural descriptions;
    it only guarantees the raw artifact is stored and reachable.
  \item \textbf{Structural normalization.} A model-assisted extraction step
    maps each ingested work item to a set of structural fields
    (representation, assumptions, operators, preserved properties, broken
    properties). Labels are proposed by the model; resolved deterministically
    against a versioned vocabulary to stable canonical identifiers. Unresolvable
    labels stay \texttt{novel\_candidate} or \texttt{ambiguous}, never coerced
    to the nearest term.
  \item \textbf{Shape-space construction.} Canonical signatures are computed
    as order-independent fingerprints over the resolved field sets. Signatures
    are clustered by mechanistic similarity; clusters are partitioned by
    observed outcome into regimes. The cluster and failure-space records are
    deterministic functions of the signatures and vocabulary version; they
    are recomputed, not curated.
  \item \textbf{Shape proposal.} A model-native \emph{compress} operation
    reads the failure-space record and proposes shape claims (candidate
    invariants) as hypotheses about what is conserved across the failure
    population. These enter with epistemic status \emph{proposed}.
  \item \textbf{Challenge lifecycle.} Each candidate enters the challenge
    lifecycle. Probe outcomes are recorded with typed evidence
    (\texttt{inadmissible} / \texttt{inapplicable} / \texttt{inconclusive} /
    \texttt{completed\_negative} / \texttt{confirmed}); status transitions
    require explicit evidence records; boundary deltas are recorded within the
    challenge record's typed evidence links and detail fields (a dedicated
    typed \texttt{boundary\_delta} persistence contract is a specified
    extension, not yet implemented). The status transition
    graph is enforced in code: a proposed claim cannot move to surviving
    without a completed challenge record, and survival requires at least one
    \texttt{completed\_negative} attack over a nonempty eligible population.
    The in-code default challenger proposes a deliberately narrower attack set
    than the seven-probe methodology contract and does not synthesize
    counterexamples; the complete seven-probe campaigns reported for N2a and
    N5 were performed as model-assisted, operator-recorded analyses with
    retained artifacts, outside the CLI challenger.
  \item \textbf{Boundary / frontier generation.} Claims in the
    \texttt{surviving} or \texttt{operator\_attested} states --- and only
    those (weakened claims are excluded by the implemented eligibility
    predicate) --- feed a frontier step: a model-native
    \emph{project} operation proposes structural deltas that cross the surviving
    boundaries. Each proposal states its target claim, claimed structural
    violation, nearest failure family, and mechanistic distance argument.
  \item \textbf{Domain projection.} The structural delta is compiled into a
    domain-native candidate (e.g., a mathematical construction, a proof
    strategy). The projection step is model-assisted and records its prompt,
    output, and the model/version that produced it.
  \item \textbf{Assessment.} Candidates are evaluated against a predeclared
    recovery rule. The system routes to the strongest decisive verifier available;
    it abstains rather than manufacturing a verdict when comparisons are
    epistemically undecidable.
  \item \textbf{Map update.} Evaluated candidates (regardless of outcome) are
    re-ingested into the corpus; their landing points update the failure-space
    and inform the next search-policy revision.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Implementation status.}
The nine layers do not share one evidentiary status, and the distinction
matters for the systems claim. Each capability above is one of:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Implemented and exercised} --- enforced in code and exercised
    by the experiments of \cref{sec:results} (ingestion, normalization,
    shape-space construction, shape proposal, the challenge state machine and
    its survival gate, frontier eligibility gating, assessment routing and
    abstention, re-ingestion);
  \item \textbf{Performed externally with retained artifacts} --- part of the
    methodology executed as model-assisted, operator-recorded analysis rather
    than by the CLI (the seven-probe N2a/N5 challenge campaigns; the blinded
    judgment-level review in Pilot-003);
  \item \textbf{Specified extension, not yet demonstrated} --- described by
    the methodology and the repository's design documents but not implemented
    as a typed contract (dedicated \texttt{boundary\_delta} persistence; the
    full self-referential loop of comparing representational bases,
    prospectively selecting a revision, and testing whether it improves
    subsequent search).
\end{itemize}
Where a capability below or in \cref{fig:newf-arch} is not tied to a cited
artifact, it should be read at the weakest of these statuses.

\subsection{Epistemic guarantees}

Three guarantees are enforced by construction, not convention:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Status monotonicity.} No epistemic status transition occurs
    without an explicit evidence record. A model cannot claim credit for
    surviving a challenge that has not been completed.
  \item \textbf{Vocabulary immutability.} Vocabulary versions are append-only;
    once a term is canonicalized it cannot be retroactively redefined. New
    concepts require a new vocabulary version, which is recorded in the
    signature lineage.
  \item \textbf{Blinding.} In benchmark mode, the system records and audits
    information flow between training and target artifacts at ingestion,
    normalization, and signature generation, refusing to proceed if leakage
    is detected.
\end{itemize}

Implementation details (SQLite migration numbers, provider configuration,
command flags) are documented in the repository and cited in the appendix.
They do not affect the theory.

% ── 7. Experimental Method ───────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Experimental Method}
\label{sec:method-exp}

\paragraph{Corpus.}
All experiments use a project-authored study corpus on the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
conjecture: whether $4/n = 1/a + 1/b + 1/c$ has a positive integer solution
for every integer $n \geq 2$. This conjecture is mathematically open, has a
rich history of structural attempts, and generates a meaningful failure
population. The corpus is synthetic and authored for the purpose of these
experiments; it is not a crawl of the mathematical literature, and no claim
about mathematical validity follows from its use.

The corpus is partitioned into a \emph{train} split (known attempts with
outcomes) and a \emph{target} split (a withheld structural move, quarantined
from all training operations). Blinding is enforced by \texttt{newf}'s leakage
audit: the system refuses to proceed if any target artifact is reachable from
the train atlas at ingestion, normalization, or signature time.

\paragraph{Predeclared assessment protocol.}
Each experiment predeclares its success criterion before generation begins.
The two experiment families use \emph{different} assessment designs, and we do
not impose a single description on them.

For the \emph{curated-feature} experiment (Pilot-003) and the M7 blinded
benchmark, the criterion is \emph{criterion recovery}: at least one generated
proposal must structurally match the withheld target move under the code-owned
recovery rule (\texttt{recovery-rule/v1}). The classification profile is pinned
per experiment: Pilot-003 was assessed under \texttt{classify/v1} (a post-hoc
\texttt{classify/v2} reassessment was recorded under a separate experiment
identity and replaces nothing); the M7 blinded benchmark runs under
\texttt{classify/v3}. The rule abstains on out-of-vocabulary wording rather
than guessing; it does not recover proposals that are mechanistically distinct
from the target.

For the \emph{discovery} experiment (Pilot-004) there is no single withheld
target to recover. Instead, a three-lane adjudication quorum classifies each
proposal occurrence (as reference-property match, defensible-novel,
unsupported, or over-merge/prohibited-promotion), and success is judged against
a predeclared \emph{composite} endpoint (criteria C1--C3 in
\cref{sec:results-pilot004}) comparing a discovery arm against a permuted
control. Reference-property recovery and novelty classification are distinct
measurements and are reported separately.

Judgment-level review is separately recorded and distinguished from rule-based
assessment throughout.

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Every experiment record is immutable and content-addressed. Frozen artifacts
live in \texttt{corpus/experiments/}; every empirical number in the results
section is traceable to a specific file and row in those artifacts.

Across both controls, the system refused to manufacture a verdict when

\subsection{Experiment D: Novel-hypothesis challenge (N2a)}
\label{sec:results-challenge}

\paragraph{Candidate.}
N2a (\emph{density averaging ceiling}): the hypothesis that two stalled
density/averaging approaches in the corpus --- es-05 (averaging via
Bombieri--Vinogradov) and es-10 (Vaughan congruence density), both
\texttt{partial\_failure} --- are subject to an intrinsic ceiling in their
rate arguments: polylogarithmic-or-slower sieve-average growth is claimed to
be structurally insufficient to force the existence of a solution,
independent of parameter optimization. The claim's scope is
$[$es-05, es-10$]$; it does not cover the corpus's other stalled mechanisms.

This hypothesis was proposed by a model during Pilot-004 and was absent from
the reference ledger (L1--L6). It was selected for end-to-end challenge on the
criterion of high quorum confidence and no obvious scope objection.

\paragraph{Challenge outcomes.}
All seven probes were executed; their evidential characters differ and are
reported per probe:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{C1} (known counterexample): none found. The corpus's other
    mechanism families (es-01, es-02, es-03, es-07, es-08) fall outside the
    claim's density/averaging scope rather than violating it.
  \item \textbf{C2} (synthetic counterexample): \emph{no falsifying synthetic
    construction was produced.} The record reasons that a violating approach
    would need polynomial (not polylogarithmic) growth of the averaged
    quantity --- i.e., it characterizes what a counterexample would require.
    This is an argument about the shape of a hypothetical construction, not a
    completed counterexample search, and the absence of a construction does
    not re-confirm the hypothesis.
  \item \textbf{C3} (success-preserving contrast): the critical contrast case
    is es-06 (\texttt{partial\_success}), assessed as a genuine contrast that
    does not falsify N2a --- its ceiling is the quadratic-residue wall, not a
    rate ceiling internal to an averaging argument.
  \item \textbf{C4} (lower abstraction --- splits?): \textbf{Partial split found.}
    es-05 (Bombieri--Vinogradov / Titchmarsh--Korobov type) exhibits a
    density-rate ceiling; es-10 (\path{confined_to_quadratic_nonresidues})
    exhibits a reach ceiling. These are distinct sub-mechanisms. A boundary
    delta $\delta_{\text{N2a}}$ was identified: \emph{rate-framing vs.\
    reach-framing of the same exhaustion argument.}
  \item \textbf{C5} (higher abstraction --- merges?): a candidate merge into a
    general ``finite-resource exhaustion'' theme was examined and assessed
    \emph{defective}: it erases the mechanistic distinction (confinement of
    reach vs.\ rate of convergence) that carries the claim's predictive
    content. No merge was performed.
  \item \textbf{C6} (sampling bias): population limited; the claim should be
    read as holding \emph{for this corpus}, not universally.
  \item \textbf{C7} (causality): the causal reading rests on source-grounded
    observations from the corpus notes about BV/BT/TK-type machinery plus a
    proposed explanation. It is not a checked mathematical argument: in
    particular, growth of an \emph{averaged} solution-count alone cannot
    establish pointwise existence, and the record's exceptional-set expression
    $N\exp[-c(\log N)^{2/3}]$ grows unboundedly (it is the \emph{density}
    ratio that vanishes). The additional assumptions connecting the averaged
    quantity to pointwise existence remain unstated; see the attributed
    reassessment appended to the challenge record.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Disposition.}
\textbf{Seven-part challenge recorded; refinement proposed; coverage
incomplete, at model-judgment strength.} N2a stands as a failure-conditioned
regularity proposal for this corpus, refined to distinguish rate-ceiling
(es-05) from reach-ceiling (es-10) sub-mechanisms. Probe coverage is mixed:
C1/C3/C4/C6 are corpus-grounded, C2 characterizes a hypothetical
construction rather than producing one, and C7 is a proposed explanation
with known unchecked mathematical steps; the synthetic-counterexample
construction (C2) and the counterexample search were not completed, so this
is not a survival across all seven probes. A child hypothesis (N2a-child-1)
was derived but not yet independently motivated by two corpus instances and
therefore not yet admitted.

\paragraph{Significance for C4.}
This experiment provides the evidence required for Claim C4: a reference-absent,
model-generated structural hypothesis was subjected to an explicit seven-probe,
model-assisted challenge --- with per-probe evidential character recorded ---
that proposed a boundary refinement. Two probes were not fully discharged
(C2, C7): the
synthetic counterexample construction was not produced and the counterexample
search was not completed, so the outcome is a recorded challenge with incomplete
coverage rather than a survival across all seven probes. Whether the hypothesis
is mathematically correct is outside the scope of this paper, and the individual
mathematical steps were not independently verified; the claim is only that the
framework can subject such a hypothesis to explicit, recorded evidentiary
challenge.

\subsection{Experiment E: Closed-loop episode and matched-budget comparison}
\label{sec:results-episode}

Experiments A--D test model-facing capabilities and are tier-4/5 evidence
throughout. Experiment E, run after the progression above
(\cref{fig:progression} is scoped to A--D), tests something different: whether
the system's map can demonstrably change a concrete next decision and whether
that change earns its cost --- with every outcome decided by a deterministic,
exact-integer checker rather than by any model. It is the paper's only
tier-2 (reproducible computation) evidence.

\paragraph{Instrument.}
An exact witness checker for single-instance Erd\H{o}s--Straus claims:
$(x,y,z)$ witnesses $4/n = 1/x + 1/y + 1/z$ iff
$4xyz = n(yz + xz + xy)$, decided over arbitrary-precision integers
(\path{internal/witness}). No floats, no tolerance, no judgment.

\paragraph{E1: preregistered two-observation episode.}
Under a typed episode protocol (preregistration, commitment, observation, and
revision records; ordering and immutability enforced at the persistence
layer; hit/miss scores derived by code from the checker verdict, never
authored), a single closed loop was run against the live blinded-benchmark
map: the instance rule (smallest prime $\geq 10^6$ congruent to $1$ modulo
$24$, a class the corpus's failure geometry marks as hard for local identity
moves; $p = 1000033$) and the first action were frozen before any
computation. Step 1 --- a local greedy 3-term truncation, exactly the
failure-side posture the map's surviving invariants flag --- was predicted
valid and was refuted exactly (the identity misses by $1$ part in $10^{38}$,
a margin no floating-point check would resolve). A recorded map revision
then required the next action to differ structurally; step 2 --- a global
divisor-lattice search, committed before execution --- produced an exact
witness at the \emph{same} first denominator where the greedy move failed.
The protocol completed with the code-derived verdict
\texttt{revision\_credit: earned}: $97$ seconds wall-clock, zero model calls.

\paragraph{E2: matched-budget two-arm comparison.}
With the protocol (instance rule, shared four-move pool, budget of three
checker submissions per instance, and decision rule) frozen and committed
before execution, twenty instances from the same class were attempted by two
arms: an undirected arm ordering cheap local moves first, and a map-guided
arm consulting the surviving \texttt{locality} invariant once per class to
order the global move first. The undirected arm produced $3/20$ verified
witnesses at $57$ submissions (cost per hit $19.0$); the map-guided arm
produced $20/20$ at $20$ submissions (cost per hit $1.0$). Under the frozen
decision rule the map earned its cost. Across the $57$ local submissions
this ordering and stopping rule produced, the local family hit $3$ times
($\approx 5.3\%$); that rate is conditioned on the frozen move ordering,
the budget, and stop-on-hit truncation --- less budget-sensitive than the
cost-per-hit contrast, but not budget-independent --- and it shows the
local family is not a strawman (it genuinely hits at three instances).

\paragraph{What E does and does not establish.}
E measures the \emph{ordering} value of the failure geometry over a fixed,
shared move pool at a fixed budget, with the contrast compressing as budget
grows (at budget four the undirected arm reaches the global move); it is a
demonstration-scale result on a single instance class. It does not measure
discovery --- the map promoted a known move; it did not find a new mechanism
family --- and it contributes no evidence about the Erd\H{o}s--Straus
conjecture itself: every witness is independently checkable by one
multiplication. Its role in this paper's argument is narrow and specific:
the decision-loop accounting that \cref{sec:superiority} formalizes
(concentration, budget, when guidance wins) is demonstrated end-to-end on
real records with code-owned scoring, at the lowest rung of the claimed
capability rather than the highest.

\section{Conditional Superiority: When Shape-Guided Concentration Wins}
\label{sec:superiority}

\noindent{\small\scshape\color{gowaccent}Status: theoretical --- no empirical
claim.} This section proves a conditional theorem and specifies a
preregistered test; it reports no measured result about GoW or the pilots
above (\cref{rem:superiority-status}).

The experiments above are exploratory and establish no statistical effect
(\cref{sec:limitations}). Experiment E (\cref{sec:results-episode}) is a
deterministically checked, demonstration-scale instance of exactly the
accounting this section formalizes --- matched budget, frozen decision rule,
cost per verified hit --- but with twenty instances of a single class and a
fixed shared move pool it is not the finite-sample certificate of
\cref{sec:superiority-cert}, and it leaves this section's illustrative
values unmeasured. This section is a separate, \emph{theoretical}
contribution: it states precisely when a shape-guided method \emph{can}
outperform a conventional comparator under a fixed resource budget, and it
specifies the finite-sample test that would establish such an advantage from
real runs. Nothing here is a result about the reported pilots; every numerical
value is an illustrative calculation from the stated model, not measured data.
The purpose is to convert ``solving by shape'' from an attractive explanation
into a falsifiable, resource-bounded claim.

\subsection{What ``outperforms'' means}
\label{sec:superiority-def}
Let $G$ be a fixed GoW implementation and $C$ a fixed comparator. For a randomly
selected task and starting history, and a total resource budget $B$, let
\[
  Y_\pi(B) =
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \text{method } \pi \text{ achieves the verified objective within } B,\\
    0, & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
\]
and define the budgeted advantage
\[
  \Delta(B) = \mathbb{E}\!\left[Y_G(B) - Y_C(B)\right].
\]
The confirmatory claim is $\Delta(B) > \delta$ for a declared practical margin
$\delta \ge 0$ over a \emph{declared task population}, with implementation
versions, resource accounting, objective, and margin all fixed before testing.
Two caveats bound the claim. First, ``conventional search'' must be a specified
implementation, not a category: no-free-lunch results
\citep{wolpert1997nofreelunch} preclude unrestricted superiority under
all-objective averaging but say nothing against advantages on \emph{structured}
populations; the useful question is which structure $G$ exploits and whether the
tested population contains it. Second, the advantage must be
\emph{resource-bounded}: if $G$ only reorganizes evidence the baseline already
receives, an unbounded baseline could reproduce it. The claim therefore lives in
metareasoning \citep{russell1991right,horvitz1988reasoning}, where computation
earns its value through better external decisions per unit cost.

\subsection{A conditional enrichment theorem}
\label{sec:superiority-thm}
This is a deliberately simplified model of \emph{one} GoW mechanism --- using a
learned shape to concentrate search --- not a theorem about every GoW
implementation. Suppose the comparator draws candidates i.i.d.\ from a
distribution $Q$ (not necessarily uniform). Let $S$ be the set of candidates
satisfying a fixed, independently checkable goal, and let $p = Q(S)$ be the
baseline per-proposal success probability. GoW identifies a preferred region
$A$ and (in this model) samples from $Q(\cdot \mid A)$. Define the
\emph{proposal-mass retention} $r = Q(A)$ and the \emph{success retention}
$\rho = Q(A \mid S)$.

\begin{proposition}[Success enrichment]
\label{prop:enrichment}
If GoW samples from $Q(\cdot \mid A)$, its per-proposal success probability is
$p_G = Q(S \mid A) = p\rho/r$, so the enrichment is
\[
  \frac{p_G}{p} = \frac{\rho}{r}.
\]
Useful geometry retains disproportionately more successful than unsuccessful
mass ($\rho > r$); a smaller region alone ($\rho \le r$) confers no advantage.
\end{proposition}

Now charge the cost of building and using the map. Let $h$ be the additional
map-building and challenge cost, $c_G$ and $c_C$ the per-candidate
generate-and-check costs, and $B$ the shared budget. Under i.i.d.\ draws with
fixed costs, the methods take $n_C = \lfloor B/c_C \rfloor$ and
$n_G = \lfloor (B-h)/c_G \rfloor$ draws, with at-least-one-success
probabilities $P_C = 1 - (1-p)^{n_C}$ and $P_G = 1 - (1 - p\rho/r)^{n_G}$.

\begin{proposition}[Exact budgeted-superiority criterion]
\label{prop:exact}
For nondegenerate probabilities,
\[
  \boxed{\;P_G > P_C \iff n_G \log\!\left(1 - \frac{p\rho}{r}\right) < n_C \log(1-p)\;}
\]
When per-draw success is small, $\log(1-x) \approx -x$ gives the interpretable
approximation
\[
  \boxed{\;\frac{\rho}{r} > \frac{c_G/c_C}{1 - h/B}\;}
\]
i.e.\ GoW wins when its enrichment (left) exceeds its cost penalty (right).
\end{proposition}

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{\emph{Hypothetical} calculations from
\cref{prop:exact}, not pilot results. Baseline $p=0.01$, $B=100$, $c_C=1$;
GoW overhead $h=20$. The comparator's success probability is
$1-0.99^{100}\approx 63.4\%$.}
\label{tab:superiority-worked}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Hypothetical GoW behavior & $r$ & $\rho$ & $c_G$ & Success within $B$ \\
\midrule
Accurate concentration            & 0.20 & 0.90 & 1 & \textbf{97.5\%} \\
Excludes too many successes        & 0.20 & 0.10 & 1 & 33.0\% \\
Accurate region, costly projection & 0.20 & 0.90 & 4 & 60.2\% \\
Conventional comparator            & ---  & ---  & 1 & 63.4\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\Cref{tab:superiority-worked} separates three distinct failure modes: the map
does not concentrate success ($\rho$ low); the map excludes useful routes; and
the map is accurate but projecting candidates from it is too expensive ($c_G$
high). The last row is the one that matters most for GoW's thesis: ``search in
shape-space'' has no economic advantage if projecting back into domain-space
consumes what it saved.

\paragraph{No hidden free sampler.}
If GoW cannot sample $A$ directly but instead generates ordinary candidates and
screens out those outside $A$, the filtering does not avoid the cost of
generating the rejected candidates. With raw generation cost $g$, shape-screen
cost $s$, and full verification cost $v$, the expected cost to first verified
success is $\mathbb{E}[T_C] = (g+v)/p$ and $\mathbb{E}[T_G] = h + (g+s+rv)/(p\rho)$.

\begin{corollary}[Filter-model cost condition]
\label{cor:filter}
Under the screening model, GoW is cheaper iff
\[
  \boxed{\;p h + g + s + r v < \rho\,(g + v)\;}
\]
With perfect retention $\rho = 1$ this is $(1-r)\,v > s + p h$: the verification
work \emph{avoided} must pay for screening and map construction.
\end{corollary}

\Cref{prop:exact,cor:filter} state the conditions under which GoW \emph{loses},
which is what makes them useful rather than promotional. They prove a
relationship among enrichment, retention, overhead, and performance; they do
\emph{not} prove that GoW can learn the right region, and conditioning is not
unique to GoW (learned proposal distributions and ordinary heuristics can
exploit the same advantage). GoW's contribution must be demonstrated in how it
\emph{obtains and uses} the concentration. For genuinely novel generation
outside the baseline's support, $Q(\cdot\mid A)$ is no longer an adequate model
and $p_G$ must be measured directly; the test below does not assume GoW behaves
as a filter.

\subsection{A finite-sample superiority certificate}
\label{sec:superiority-cert}
The theorem explains a possible advantage; establishing it requires paired runs
under a frozen design. Sample $n$ independent task-and-history episodes from the
declared population and run both methods on each with the same starting evidence
and resource cap. Let $D_i = Y_{G,i} - Y_{C,i} \in \{-1,0,1\}$ and
$\widehat{\Delta} = \tfrac1n\sum_i D_i$. Methods may adapt arbitrarily
\emph{within} an episode; the argument requires only that episode \emph{pairs}
be independent under the frozen design.

\begin{proposition}[One-sided lower confidence bound]
\label{prop:hoeffding}
Since $D_i$ lies in an interval of width $2$, Hoeffding's inequality
\citep{hoeffding1963probability} gives
$\Pr(\widehat{\Delta} - \Delta \ge \varepsilon) \le \exp(-n\varepsilon^2/2)$, so
at a \emph{fixed, prespecified} $n$,
\[
  \boxed{\;L_\alpha = \widehat{\Delta} - \sqrt{\tfrac{2\log(1/\alpha)}{n}}\;}
  \qquad \Pr(L_\alpha \le \Delta) \ge 1-\alpha.
\]
A practical margin $\delta$ is established when $L_\alpha > \delta$. This bounds
the procedure's probability of overstating the advantage at $\alpha$; it is not
a posterior probability that GoW is better.
\end{proposition}

As an \emph{illustrative} instance (not measured): a paired table of $300$
episodes with $105$ both-succeed, $90$ GoW-only, $30$ comparator-only, and $75$
neither gives $\widehat{\Delta} = (90-30)/300 = 0.20$ and, at $\alpha=0.05$,
$L_{0.05} = 0.20 - \sqrt{2\log 20 / 300} \approx 0.0587$ --- a conservative
bound still exceeding a five-point margin. A more efficient test of the
no-advantage null uses only the discordant pairs: conditional on $120$
discordant episodes, the count favoring GoW is $\mathrm{Binomial}(120,\tfrac12)$
under equal success probabilities, the exact paired form associated with
McNemar's test \citep{mcnemar1947note}; for this table
$\Pr\{\mathrm{Binomial}(120,\tfrac12) \ge 90\} \approx 1.89\times 10^{-8}$. The
small $p$-value addresses equality, not the five-point margin --- which is why an
effect estimate and confidence bound belong beside it.

First, $776$ stochastic reruns of \emph{one} task are not $776$ independent task

inspecting the result and stopping at a favorable moment, the fixed-$n$ bound is

another anytime-valid method \citep{howard2021timeuniform}.

\subsection{An experiment that tests GoW, not extra assistance}
\label{sec:superiority-exp}
The confirmatory design (detailed as a preregistration in the project
repository, \path{corpus/experiments/superiority-preregistration/}) makes one
primary comparison and one diagnostic ablation:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Conventional adaptive search:} same model, raw history, tools,
    checker, and total budget; ordinary generation, feedback, reflection, and
    revision (not prohibited from noticing interactions or reasoning about
    failures).
  \item \textbf{GoW-guided search:} explicit representation selection, shape
    hypotheses, challenge, structural-intervention selection, projection, and
    feedback.
  \item \textbf{Curation-only ablation:} comparable organization/retrieval
    assistance \emph{without} the explicit shape-to-action policy, isolating
    whether any gain comes from search control rather than convenient history.
\end{itemize}
The primary endpoint uses a bounded domain with executable assessment (e.g.\
program-repair or small synthesis tasks with an exact checker over a declared
finite specification); Erd\H{o}s--Straus remains a motivating case, but
``resembles a research direction'' is not interchangeable with checked task
completion. Held-out \emph{task structures} (not merely renamed identifiers)
must include both transferable-structure and structure-absent cases. The
resource account charges GoW's normalization, mapping, hypothesis generation,
challenge, projection, unsuccessful attempts, and checking; human intervention
is equalized, prohibited during evaluation, or explicitly counted as part of the
method; cold-start and amortized costs are reported separately. The primary
outcome $Y=0$ means ``no verified objective within budget'' --- not ``false'' or
``impossible'' --- with invalid output, incomplete verification, timeout, and
ordinary unsuccessful search preserved as secondary dispositions and never
dropped from the denominator. A budget-capped efficiency measure
$\mathbb{E}[\min(T_\pi, B)]$ (with $T_\pi = \infty$ on failure) avoids comparing
speed only among solved tasks.

\subsection{What would make the evidence specifically about GoW}
\label{sec:superiority-attribution}
A significant result establishes a difference between implementations, not the
\emph{explanation} of that difference. The GoW account is supported to the
extent these line up: \textbf{action relevance} (changing or removing the
inferred shape changes the next attempted work); \textbf{prospective enrichment}
(shape-guided proposals succeed more often on outcomes not used to choose the
shape); \textbf{preservation} (the gain is not obtained by quietly changing the
goal or dropping hard cases); \textbf{net efficiency} (the gain survives charging
map and projection costs); and \textbf{adaptive value} (revised maps improve
\emph{later} decisions, not merely fit earlier failures). On a bounded domain,
$r$, $\rho$, and the cost of using $A$ can be estimated directly and compared
against \cref{prop:exact}'s prediction, yielding an interpretable result even
when GoW loses (e.g.\ ``the map concentrated success, but projection overhead
erased the benefit''). A win that vanishes against the curation-only ablation
would locate the contribution in evidence organization rather than the search
policy. The provenance required for this attribution --- provider/configuration
metadata and raw request/response records --- is already produced by
\texttt{newf} (\cref{sec:system}).

\begin{remark}[Status of this section]
\label{rem:superiority-status}
\Cref{prop:enrichment,prop:exact,cor:filter,prop:hoeffding} are mathematical
statements under their stated assumptions. The empirical premise --- that a fixed
GoW implementation achieves sufficient enrichment net of overhead on a declared
population --- is \emph{not} established by this paper and is not claimed. The
central proposition is conditional: GoW outperforms when its learned organization
of work increases verified success per unit total effort enough to offset the
cost of building and using that organization.
\end{remark}

% ── 10. Discussion ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the evidence does and does not establish}

The four experiments form a progression, not an independence sample. Experiment A
establishes epistemic behavior (the system abstains rather than inventing
structure). Experiment B establishes a directional capability finding (shape-guided
context produced the only full-recovery proposal, visible to a blinded reviewer
but not to the automatic rule). Experiment C establishes that reference-absent
shape generation occurs (the predeclared effectiveness endpoint was not met, but
the constituent capability was demonstrated). Experiment D establishes that a
model-generated reference-absent hypothesis can be subjected to an explicit
seven-part challenge with per-probe evidential character recorded: a
refinement was proposed (a boundary delta splitting one structural ceiling
into two sub-mechanisms), two of seven probes were not fully discharged, and
the outcome is a recorded challenge with incomplete coverage --- not a
completed lifecycle or a survived hypothesis --- informative regardless of
that incompleteness.

What the progression does not establish: that GoW reliably outperforms undirected
generation (one pilot does not support this), that the found structural hypotheses
are mathematically correct (no formal verification was attempted), or that the
framework generalizes beyond the Erd\H{o}s--Straus study corpus.

\subsection{The interesting distinction}

The most important epistemic lesson from Pilot-004 is the distinction between
two results that superficially look like the same negative result:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Method reliability endpoint not met} (majority-of-runs
    reference recovery and the error-count comparison both failed):
    \emph{negative} result.
  \item \textbf{Reference-absent shape generation observed}: \emph{positive}
    capability finding within the negative-effectiveness run.
\end{itemize}

These are compatible. The effectiveness endpoint tested whether the method is
\emph{reliable}; the capability finding recorded whether a specific operation
\emph{can occur}. A single demonstration of reference-absent shape generation,
even inside a negative-effectiveness pilot, is evidence that the capability
exists. It does not establish that the method is useful at scale.

\subsection{Micro-failures and recursive geometry}

The N2a challenge (Experiment D) produced a boundary delta that decomposed one
``structural ceiling'' into two distinct sub-mechanisms. This is a micro-instance
of the GoW loop running at finer granularity: the challenge step itself is a
recursive cartography pass, and its productive output is not merely a verdict
but a sharper map. The delta --- rate-ceiling (es-05) vs.\ reach-ceiling (es-10)
--- emerged during the challenge step rather than from the initial mapping. We
did not run an ablation isolating challenge from other operations, so we do not
claim the distinction was unobtainable without challenge; we report only that,
in this instance, it surfaced during challenge. This is suggestive that the
challenge step can add map-resolution value independent of whether the
challenged hypothesis ultimately survives, not a demonstration of its necessity.

\subsection{Human use of GoW}

Nothing in the methodology requires a model. A practitioner with domain
expertise can execute the Map step manually (by listing and describing prior
attempts), the Challenge step by applying the seven probes with judgment, and
the Boundary step by identifying what structurally separates the better from the
worse outcomes. Models add throughput, not authority. The epistemic model is
designed to keep that distinction clear: a model-generated structural hypothesis
is a \emph{proposed} claim, not a \emph{discovered} one, regardless of how
convincing the argument sounds.

\subsection{Complement geometry: the dual reading}
\label{sec:complement}

The forward geometry developed in \cref{sec:semantic} reads shape from the
structure of completed work---what is \emph{present} in shape-space. A dual
reading is available: infer shape from the regions of possibility-space that
accumulated work \emph{excludes}.

Let $\Omega$ denote an ambient possibility-space of structurally plausible
work on a problem. Each work item $W_i$ imposes a constraint $C_i$ on this
space---a mechanism shown not to work, a scope demonstrated to be insufficient,
a combination established as unproductive. The surviving space after $n$ pieces
of work is:
\[
\Omega_W \;=\; \Omega \;\setminus\; \bigcup_{i=1}^{n} F_i
\]
where each $F_i$ is the region excluded by the evidence from $W_i$. As work
accumulates, the residual region $\Omega_W$ may acquire a non-trivial
shape---determined not by any single attempt but by the \emph{collective
constraints} of all attempts.

We call this the \textbf{anti-vacuum}: a region that is unoccupied by prior work
but structurally determined by everything that surrounds it.

\begin{remark}[Complementarity Principle]
Work informs search in two ways: through the structures instantiated by completed
attempts (\emph{forward geometry}) and through the regions of possibility-space
those attempts exclude or constrain (\emph{complement geometry}). Sufficiently
structured negative evidence can induce a residual geometry whose conserved
properties specify candidate directions not instantiated by prior Work.
\end{remark}

The forward and complement readings yield different objects:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
& \textbf{Forward geometry} & \textbf{Complement geometry} \\
\midrule
Input & occupied work & excluded possibility-space \\
Yields & clusters, invariants, regimes & forbidden regions, cavities, corridors \\
Key object & conserved shape & residual structural requirements \\
Generator prompt & ``cross this boundary'' & ``occupy this constrained void'' \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The complement view reinterprets the three outcome types as different kinds of
geometric force:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Failure} imposes \emph{exclusion}---structural constraints on
    what remains possible.
  \item \textbf{Partial success} imposes \emph{gradient}---directional evidence
    about where improvement lies within the surviving region.
  \item \textbf{Success} imposes \emph{attractor evidence}---direct constraint
    on what structural features a solution possesses.
\end{itemize}

Under this reading, a heavily worked problem with many diverse, well-characterized
failures is \emph{plausibly} highly constrained rather than hopeless. One might
hope that the residual region concentrates --- informally, $\dim(\Omega_n) \ll
\dim(\Omega)$ --- but this is a proposed possibility, not an established
consequence: excluding possibilities does not by itself lower dimension, and a
smaller residual region can retain the original dimension. Whether accumulated
constraints reduce the effective dimension of the residual region, and under
what conditions, is left open here (see the discussion of $\Omega$ as a research
problem below). Under favorable constraints, community resistance may become an
asset rather than a discouragement, because the residual cavity may be
increasingly well defined.

This gives the three modes of failed work:
\begin{quote}
Conventional search treats failed work as \emph{consumed effort}.\\
Geometry of Work treats failed work as \emph{material}.\\
Complement geometry treats failed work as \emph{pressure}.
\end{quote}

A formal \emph{constraint-collapse operator} $\mathcal{C}(\Omega \mid W) \to R$
that computes the residual region and extracts its structural properties is a
natural next primitive, but remains a hypothesis: we have not implemented or
tested it. The complement view is stated here as a theoretically motivated
extension, not as a demonstrated capability.

\subsubsection*{Connection to the void concept in the forward geometry}

The forward geometry (\cref{sec:semantic}) already contains \emph{voids}: the
component $V \subset \mathcal{G}$ of unoccupied or under-sampled structural
combinations. Complement geometry extends this idea in two directions.

First, forward voids are \emph{empirical}: they are the structural combinations
that the observed work population happens not to occupy. They may be absent
because they are unproductive, because they are difficult, or simply because
no one has attempted them. The forward reading does not distinguish these cases.

Complement geometry attempts to distinguish them by asking what
constraint each work item imposes on the surrounding possibility-space. A void
that persists under many independent, well-characterized failures --- each of
which excludes overlapping regions of $\Omega$ --- is qualitatively different
from a void that merely lacks samples. The former is a \emph{constrained cavity};
the latter is a \emph{sampling gap}. The forward geometry alone cannot make
this distinction; the complement view provides the machinery.

Second, forward geometry proposes \emph{structural deltas} that cross observed
boundaries. Complement geometry proposes structural moves that \emph{occupy
constrained cavities} --- regions whose shape is determined by what surrounds
them rather than by what has been tried inside them. The two proposal
strategies are complementary: the first targets shape claims that hold across
failure regimes; the second targets shape claims that are \emph{forced by
external constraint} even though no attempt has instantiated them.

\paragraph{Why $\Omega$ is a research problem.}
The complement view relies on an ambient possibility-space $\Omega$ of
``structurally plausible work.'' Defining this space rigorously is nontrivial:
it must be broad enough to contain the work that has not been tried, narrow
enough to be structurally meaningful, and constructible enough that the
constraint-collapse operator $\mathcal{C}(\Omega \mid W)$ has a computable
output. In practice, $\Omega$ may need to be defined implicitly, via the
vocabulary of the forward geometry --- $\Omega$ as the closure of all
type-consistent combinations of the vocabulary's canonical labels. This is a
theoretical open problem that must be addressed before complement geometry can
be operationalized.

% ── 11. Related Work ─────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Several research traditions inform GoW, and a careful comparison is necessary
to identify where the contribution lies. The main finding of the comparison is
that, \emph{among the traditions surveyed here}, none combines all of the
following: (1) representing prior
attempts as structured objects, (2) conditioning shape claims on outcome class,
(3) inferring conserved structure across a \emph{population} of attempts, (4)
defining boundaries between outcome regimes, (5) generating next work from the
boundary analysis, and (6) tracking epistemic provenance of structural claims.
We claim this conjunction as a proposed synthesis relative to the surveyed
work; we do not claim to have established its absence across every field.

\paragraph{Metaheuristics and search-space methods.}
Simulated annealing and genetic algorithms, as originally
formulated~\citep{kirkpatrick1983optimization,
goldberg1989genetic}, operate on a
\emph{solution} space with a fitness function. They implicitly use failure (low fitness) to bias search
but do not construct a structured description of \emph{why} attempts fail.
(Later model-building metaheuristics such as estimation-of-distribution
algorithms and CMA-ES do fit an explicit probabilistic model of the
promising search region each generation, a coarser structural
characterization than GoW's regime/boundary/epistemic apparatus but closer
than SA/GA; we do not survey them in depth here.)
The geometry is over solution encodings, not over structural descriptions of
attempt history. GoW is complementary: it adds a structural-description layer
that can inform the design of the search space itself, not just the trajectory
through it.

\paragraph{Novelty search and quality-diversity.}
Novelty search \citep{lehman2011abandoning} and MAP-Elites \citep{mouret2015illuminating}
archive \emph{behavioral} descriptors and reward diversity. FunSearch's
underlying search mechanism (below) is an evolutionary program database with
diversity-preserving island maintenance, mechanistically closer to this
family than to hypothesis-driven discovery; we return to it here for that
reason. The spaces novelty search and MAP-Elites
maintain are over behavior (input/output profiles), not over mechanism structure.
GoW's structural shapes are outcome-blind mechanism descriptions; its regimes
are outcome-conditioned; its epistemic lifecycle has no counterpart in novelty
search. There is no challenge, no boundary hypothesis, and no domain projection
in the quality-diversity tradition.

\paragraph{Case-based reasoning and analogical reasoning.}
Classical case-based reasoning \citep{kolodner1993case} retrieves structurally similar
cases to adapt for new problems, using a similarity metric to find
\emph{individual} similar cases at query time, not to characterize a \emph{population} of
failures. A closer neighbor is the case-base maintenance / competence-model
line of work, which does analyze a case base as a population --- computing
coverage groups and competence footprints over the whole library rather than
per-query retrieval alone. GoW's distinction from that closer neighbor is
that competence models characterize retrieval coverage of a case base; they
do not condition cases on outcome regime or generate boundary-crossing
structural deltas with typed epistemic state. Structure-mapping theories of
analogy \citep{gentner1983structure} compare the relational structure of a
\emph{pair} of situations rather than their surface features --- the same
structural-comparison move GoW makes, but pairwise/one-to-one rather than
population/many-to-many, and without an outcome-conditioned regime or
epistemic-state lifecycle. GoW does not retrieve or align pairwise; it mines
a population. The governing question is not
``what does this problem look like?'' but ``what is conserved across the
failure population, and what structural move would violate it?''

\paragraph{Automated scientific discovery.}
Systems in the BACON/Eureka tradition \citep{langley1987scientific} discover
laws from data about physical objects. The AI Scientist \citep{lu2024aiscientist}
generates hypotheses and runs experiments. FunSearch~\citep{romera2024funsearch}
proposes candidate programs with an LLM and selects them against an external
combinatorial-evaluation harness, achieving new results on specific
mathematical problems; mechanistically, as noted above, its evolutionary
program-database search is closer to the quality-diversity family than to
BACON/Eureka's measurement-driven law induction, though its target domain
(mathematical constructions) places it in this paragraph by surface topic.
The target of discovery in BACON/Eureka and The AI Scientist is
\emph{object-level} patterns in measurements (or, for FunSearch, an
object-level extremal construction). GoW's target is the geometry of
\emph{prior work on a problem}: a meta-level object. The two can coexist
(GoW can use scientific discovery methods, including FunSearch-style
proposer/evaluator loops, inside a projection step), but they answer
different questions.

\paragraph{Interactive theorem proving, learned proof search, and conjecture generation.}
Interactive theorem provers (Lean, Isabelle, Metamath)
\citep{harrison2014history} rely on human- or model-authored tactic scripts
rather than autonomous proof search; fully automated theorem provers (ATP in
the narrower sense, e.g.\ resolution/superposition/SMT systems) are a
distinct class not separately surveyed here. Conjecturing systems (Graffiti,
TxGraffiti) \citep{fajtlowicz1988graffiti}
generate conjectures from graph properties. Some recent theorem-proving work
explicitly
learns from failed search paths: TrialMaster \citep{an2024trialmaster} trains
on complete proof trees including failed tactic sequences and backtracking
instructions, reporting higher success on unseen theorems at lower search cost
than models trained on correct paths alone. AlphaGeometry \citep{trinh2024alphageometry},
GPT-f's expert iteration \citep{polu2020generative}, and HOList's
reinforcement-learned proof search \citep{bansal2019holist} likewise generate and
consume large populations of failed forward-deduction or proof-search
attempts, within a single formal system, toward training-signal extraction ---
failures are generated, filtered, and folded into model weights or a symbolic
engine, not retained as individually inspectable, typed, provenance-carrying
objects. So the claim that theorem-proving failures are
categorically unused is false; the narrower and defensible distinction is what
is being learned \emph{from}, and in what form the result is kept.
TrialMaster's, AlphaGeometry's, GPT-f's, and HOList's object of learning is proof-tree
or deduction-chain
structure within a single formal system, indexed by tactic sequences,
backtracking signals, or deduction steps inside one proof search, and the
outcome is training signal folded into a model or engine. GoW's failure population is a
heterogeneous set of \emph{mathematical approaches} --- distinct mechanisms,
often from different sub-fields, compared at the level of preserved structural
properties rather than shared proof steps, and retained as individually
inspectable typed claims rather than consumed into weights.
Both treat failure as informative;
they differ in what object failure is measured over (a proof tree/deduction
chain vs.\ a
cross-attempt mechanism population) and in whether the resulting structure is
a typed, provenance-carrying record subject to explicit epistemic-state
transitions (GoW) or a training signal folded into model weights or a symbolic
search engine (TrialMaster, AlphaGeometry, GPT-f).

\paragraph{Program synthesis and counterexample-guided abstraction refinement.}
CEGIS \citep{solar-lezama2008program} and CEGAR \citep{clarke2000counterexample}
are the closest neighbors. Both use counterexamples to refine an abstraction:
CEGAR's original object is an abstract model built for verification/model
checking, refined by spurious counterexamples the abstraction admits but the
concrete system does not; CEGIS refines a candidate program against
counterexamples from a verifier, often an SMT solver such as
Z3~\citep{demoura2008z3}. In both, the refinement target is one
abstraction (of one model or one synthesis target), and a counterexample
against it is consumed directly to sharpen that same abstraction. GoW's
proposed distinction is narrower than ``GoW mines a population, CEGAR does
not'': it is that GoW treats a heterogeneous population of \emph{approach-level
Work} as a persistent typed object whose cross-attempt structural hypotheses
carry provenance and epistemic state (proposed / challenged / surviving /
weakened / falsified), rather than refining a single abstraction in place. In
CEGIS the abstraction space is given; in GoW it is inferred from work
descriptions and can itself be re-represented. In both, a counterexample is
productive; in GoW, any challenge outcome (including a boundary delta that
splits rather than falsifies a hypothesis) is a structured measurement of
terrain, retained with the hypothesis's history rather than only used to patch
the current abstraction.

\paragraph{Meta-learning and learning-to-search.}
Meta-learning in the modern task-distribution sense \citep{finn2017maml} learns across
tasks to improve on new tasks quickly; the meta-space is \emph{task-indexed}.
Schmidhuber's self-referential learning \citep{schmidhuber1987evolutionary} is
an earlier, informal proposal about a learner modifying its own learning
algorithm, not a task-distribution formalism in this sense --- we cite it for
the self-modifying lineage, not for the task-indexed framing.
GoW's geometry is \emph{attempt-indexed}: the same task, many attempts.
Meta-learning assumes a distribution over tasks and optimizes for fast adaptation;
GoW assumes a history of work on one hard problem and optimizes for informative
next moves.

\paragraph{Failure analysis and root-cause analysis.}
Engineering failure analysis \citep{anderson2005root} diagnoses failures and,
in standard practice, also proposes corrective and preventive actions ---
it is not purely diagnostic. It shares GoW's respect for failure as data and its
attention to structural patterns across failures. The distinction is narrower than
``diagnostic, not generative'': RCA's corrective actions target a
\emph{single} diagnosed failure mode to prevent its recurrence, not a
cross-population conserved-shape boundary derived from many heterogeneous
prior attempts with typed, tracked epistemic status. GoW adds the
population-level, epistemically-typed search direction.

\paragraph{Design-space exploration.}
Multi-objective DSE, in its classical parametric form
\citep{palermo2004dse}, searches a parameter space with
known, fixed dimensions; later representation-adaptive and surrogate-guided DSE
variants exist and are not surveyed in depth here. GoW's shape-space dimensions are inferred, not given. Classical DSE
assumes the space is parametric and fixed; GoW allows the representation itself
to move (normalizing and abstracting as the geometry is refined).

\paragraph{LLM self-reflection, debate, and process reward models.}
Reflexion \citep{shinn2023reflexion} converts feedback into verbal
self-reflections stored in an episodic memory buffer and reused to improve
decisions on \emph{subsequent} trials; debate \citep{irving2018debate} has models
challenge each other's generations. Process reward models
\citep{lightman2023process} supervise individual reasoning steps. Reflexion is
therefore not confined to a single attempt --- it explicitly carries reflections
across trials. The distinction we draw is representational rather than one of
adaptivity: Reflexion's carried object is natural-language self-feedback scoped
to an agent's own task episodes, whereas GoW maintains a typed, provenance-carrying
geometry of \emph{many} attempts (across models and sessions) with explicit
epistemic status and an adversarial challenge contract, not owned by any single
model. We do not claim these methods fail to persist information; we claim a
different persisted object and evidence discipline.

\paragraph{Lakatos and the logic of mathematical discovery.}
Lakatos's \emph{Proofs and Refutations} \citep{lakatos1976proofs} is the
most relevant philosophical ancestor for GoW's \emph{epistemic} dialectic ---
conjecture, refutation, and revision as the engine of mathematical knowledge.
Lakatos describes mathematical
knowledge as evolving through a dialectic of conjecture and refutation, with
monster-barring and exception-incorporation as the tools; mechanistically,
the case studies in \emph{Proofs and Refutations} refine a single evolving
conjecture (the Euler polyhedron formula) against a sequence of
counterexamples, which is structurally closer to CEGAR's single-abstraction
refinement (above) than to GoW's population-mining move. GoW's
distinction relative to Lakatos is therefore the same one drawn relative to
CEGAR/CEGIS: GoW additionally treats a heterogeneous \emph{population} of
independently-generated approaches as the object mined for conserved
structure, rather than refining one conjecture serially. GoW operationalizes
a computational version of Lakatos's epistemic dialectic, replacing Lakatos's informal
philosophical analysis with typed, persisted, adversarially challenged structural
claims, applied here across a population rather than to one conjecture's
history. The key lift is that GoW makes the process machine-executable and
makes the failure population a first-class computational object.
Where Lakatos is retrospective and dialectical (refining one conjecture after
refutation), Polya's heuristics \citep{polya1945solve} are prescriptive and
generative for an individual solver (find a related problem, specialize,
generalize, work backward from the goal). GoW's distinction from Polya is the
same population-level move: Polya's heuristics operate on one solver's search
for one problem; GoW mines conserved structure across a population of
distinct attempts.

\paragraph{Instance space analysis.}
ISA \citep{smith2014instancespace} characterizes algorithm performance by
constructing a geometry over problem instances. GoW constructs a geometry over
\emph{solution attempts} for a fixed problem. ISA requires a known algorithm
portfolio and a problem generator; GoW requires a corpus of prior work and a
structural vocabulary. The two framings are dual: ISA asks which algorithm to
use for which problem; GoW asks which structural move to make on which problem.

\paragraph{The narrow novelty claim.}
None of the traditions surveyed above --- including, on this revision,
AlphaGeometry/GPT-f's within-formalism failure populations and the
case-based-reasoning competence-model literature's case-base coverage
analysis, both added above specifically because they are the closest
population-level neighbors to GoW's own mechanism --- combines outcome-conditioned regime construction,
conserved-structure inference over a population of heterogeneous attempts, an
explicit boundary concept, structural delta generation, and epistemic provenance
of structural claims. The conjunction of these properties --- particularly the
use of a \emph{population} of heterogeneous work as the primary object,
with representation itself allowed to move --- is the claim GoW makes on the
literature. It is a narrow claim, and it is the claim we hold; we do not claim
to have surveyed every population-mining or competence-analysis method that
could bear on it.

% ── 12. Limitations and Threats to Validity ──────────────────────────
\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Single domain.}
All experiments use a project-authored corpus on a single open number-theory
problem. No claim about GoW's effectiveness on other domains or problem types
is supported by this evidence. Whether the shape vocabulary, challenge
operations, and boundary concept transfer to other disciplines remains an
empirical question.

\paragraph{Compressed development window.}
The line of inquiry began at 2026-09-10 03:49 (UTC$-$7, author-reported),
with an initial focus on extracting shared structure from histories of
unsuccessful attempts; the broader formulation, the \texttt{newf}
implementation, the study corpus, all experiments, and this manuscript
emerged within a compressed window of a few days, with commit-anchored
elapsed-time entries collected in \path{paper/HISTORY.md} (the most recent
entry at manuscript freeze time was $+38.1$h; remediation passes after that
point are not included in that figure). The inception
timestamp attaches to the initial insight only --- the present framework is
not retrodated to it. Three consequences follow. First, the corpus, the
system that analyzes it, and the evaluation were authored by the same
operator-plus-model process in the same window --- the strongest form of the
practitioner-curation threat that falsification condition F5 tests. Second,
no longitudinal claim is possible: nothing here shows the method's outputs
survive contact with time, independent replication, or an external corpus.
Third, the compression is a throughput observation about the development
process, not evidence for any scientific claim in this paper, and it is
never cited as such.

\paragraph{LLM-dependent structural descriptions.}
Structural normalization is model-assisted. Different model families may produce
different structural descriptions for the same piece of work, yielding different
geometries. We do not have a model-independent procedure for structural
description, and the effect of model variation on geometry stability has not
been measured.

\paragraph{Same-model-family quorum.}
The adversarial challenge and the quorum adjudication in Pilot-004 both used
models from the same family as the original generation. Cross-model-family
agreement is a stronger evidence tier; we have not achieved it. Results labeled
as tier-5 (single-model judgment) or tier-4 (same-family quorum) should be
read accordingly.

\paragraph{Curated corpus.}
The Erd\H{o}s--Straus study corpus was authored for these experiments. It is
not a random sample of the mathematical literature, and it is not guaranteed
to represent the full diversity of approaches. Publication bias, selection bias,
and the researchers' own familiarity with the problem structure all influence
the corpus and therefore the geometry.

\paragraph{No statistical power.}
One pilot does not establish a statistical effect. Pilot-003's B3 recovery
and Pilot-004's reference-absent shape generation are single-pilot observations.
The predeclared aggregate criterion in Pilot-004 was not met; the individual
capability findings are not a substitute for aggregate power. The conditional
theorem and finite-sample certificate in \cref{sec:superiority} are theoretical:
they state when superiority \emph{would} hold and how it \emph{would} be
measured, but they are not evidence that the present implementation attains it.

\paragraph{Projection bottleneck.}
In every experiment, domain projection was model-assisted and produced candidates
that were mechanistically distinct from the withheld target under the code-owned
recovery rule. Whether this reflects an inherent limitation of the projection
step or an artifact of the specific target is unknown.

\paragraph{No convergence guarantee.}
GoW does not guarantee that the geometry converges to a useful representation,
that challenge produces stable boundaries, or that repeated application of the
loop improves the proposal quality in any measurable sense. These are research
questions, not established properties.

\paragraph{Experiment D's challenge lifecycle was not executed by the \texttt{newf} CLI.}
The seven-probe challenge campaigns reported for N2a and N5
(\cref{sec:results-challenge}) were performed as model-assisted,
operator-recorded analyses with retained artifacts, outside the CLI
challenger (\cref{sec:system}); the in-code default challenger implements a
deliberately narrower attack set and does not currently synthesize
counterexamples. Claim C4 is therefore evidence about what the seven-probe
methodology contract can show when executed manually under the same
epistemic discipline the system enforces, not evidence that the shipped
\texttt{newf} binary currently automates that full contract end to end.
Automating the complete seven-probe challenge inside the CLI remains future
work (\cref{sec:future}).

% ── 13. Future Work ──────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{Second domain.}
Demonstrating GoW on a domain other than Erd\H{o}s--Straus is the most
important step toward generality. Candidate domains include open combinatorics
conjectures, program synthesis benchmarks, and scientific hypothesis refinement.

\paragraph{Statistical evaluation.}
\Cref{sec:superiority} states the conditional advantage and the finite-sample
certificate that would establish superiority; the missing piece is the data.
Experiment E's matched-budget comparison (\cref{sec:results-episode}) is a
first deterministic data point of the right \emph{shape} --- frozen protocol,
matched budget, exact checking --- but not the specified experiment: it lacks
the adaptive-search baseline, the curation-only ablation, held-out task
structures, and any statistical form. The
next empirical target is the preregistered paired experiment specified there and
in \path{corpus/experiments/superiority-preregistration/}: a matched
adaptive-search baseline, a GoW-guided arm, and a curation-only ablation over a
bounded domain with executable verification, with a frozen budget, comparator,
and analysis. This would convert the present directional, pilot-scale
observations into a measurable effect estimate with a one-sided lower confidence
bound (\cref{prop:hoeffding}), and would let the observed advantage be checked
against the enrichment/overhead prediction of \cref{prop:exact}.

\paragraph{Formal convergence analysis.}
Does the challenge-refine loop converge to a fixed point? Under what conditions
does repeated boundary extraction reduce search diameter? These questions admit
formal analysis in a simplified model.

\paragraph{Human-in-the-loop GoW.}
GoW does not require a model at every step. A practitioner with domain expertise
can execute the Map, Challenge, and Boundary steps manually. Evaluating
human-assisted GoW against fully automated GoW would clarify where models add
value and where domain expertise dominates.

\paragraph{Micro-failure geometry.}
This paper treats work items at the granularity of complete approaches. Within
a single approach, step-level failures (failed lemmas, failed case analyses)
constitute a finer-grained failure population with its own geometry. The same
loop applies recursively at this level.

\paragraph{Complement geometry.}
The complement view introduced in \cref{sec:complement} is theoretically
motivated but not yet implemented or tested. Formalizing the constraint-collapse
operator $\mathcal{C}(\Omega \mid W) \to R$ and evaluating whether complement
geometry identifies directions that forward geometry misses is a natural
follow-on experiment.

\paragraph{Search-policy mutation.}
The policy-mutation step (\texttt{policy mutate}) in \texttt{newf} is
implemented but not yet evaluated. Measuring whether policy-driven search
outperforms static context supply over multiple rounds would test whether the
geometry's feedback loop is productive.

% ── 14. Conclusion ───────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced \emph{Geometry of Work} (GoW), a discipline for treating the
history of problem-solving attempts as a structured search space whose geometry
contains useful information about how search is failing or progressing. The
core thesis is:

\begin{quote}
When repeated Work stops producing information, treat the Work itself as data.
\end{quote}

We made four deliberate claims. Conceptually (C1), a history of work can be
represented as a shape-space with regions, boundaries, and conserved properties.
Methodologically (C2), the \textsc{Map $\to$ Boundary $\to$ Move $\to$
Project $\to$ Measure} loop provides a disciplined way to choose informative
next work rather than continuing linear search. As a systems contribution (C3),
\texttt{newf} operationalizes GoW with typed provenance, epistemic state
tracking, adversarial challenge, and domain projection --- demonstrating that
fuzzy model-native operations and deterministic evidence can coexist without
silently promoting claims. Empirically and narrowly (C4), in project-authored
Erd\H{o}s--Straus study corpora, large language models proposed structural
regularities absent from the reference ledger, shape-guided context produced the
only proposal judged by a blinded reviewer a full criterion recovery of a
withheld structural
move (identifier masking, not full concealment of the experimental condition,
was achieved), and one reference-absent hypothesis was subjected to a seven-part,
model-assisted challenge that proposed a boundary refinement, with counterexample
coverage left incomplete and the mathematical steps not independently verified.
Beyond the C4 progression, a preregistered closed decision loop and a
matched-budget comparison --- the paper's only reproducible-computation
evidence --- demonstrated at small scale that a recorded map revision changed
the committed next action and that the map's ordering of a fixed move pool
earned its checker-submission cost under a frozen decision rule at a frozen
per-instance budget.
The experiments
establish epistemic behavior and constituent capabilities; they do not establish
mathematical validity, general discovery effectiveness, or autonomous theorem
solving.

The framework's epistemic model is as important as the geometric methodology.
Without explicit separation of shape from conditioning, regularity from
obstruction, model judgment from verification, and absence from negation, a
framework for learning from failures is simply a platform for laundering
speculation into authority. GoW's epistemic architecture makes these
separations load-bearing rather than advisory.

The nearest intellectual ancestors --- Lakatos's \emph{Proofs and Refutations},
CEGIS/CEGAR, and instance space analysis --- each contribute one component.
Among these, none combines outcome-conditioned regime construction, conserved-structure
inference over a heterogeneous attempt population, an explicit challenge
lifecycle, and epistemic provenance of structural claims. The conjunction is
the contribution.

GoW does not solve hard problems. It makes the structure of the search more
legible, the failures more informative, and the next move more motivated.
Whether the approach transfers beyond Erd\H{o}s--Straus, whether it produces
measurably better proposals than undirected generation, and whether the geometry
converges to something stable are open questions this paper deliberately leaves
open. It presents a theory, a methodology, a working instrument, and the
exploratory evidence sufficient to make the framework refereeable.

\section{Formal Definitions}
\label{app:formal}

This appendix consolidates the definitions introduced across
\cref{sec:semantic,sec:epistemic,sec:method} into a single reference.

\begin{definition}[Work item, restated]
$W_i = (S_i, O_i, E_i)$: structural description, observed outcome, evidence.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural shape, restated]
Outcome-blind description with fields:
\emph{representation}, \emph{assumptions}, \emph{operators},
\emph{preserved properties}, \emph{axes} (mechanistic-diversity coordinates).
Shapes are canonicalized to order-independent fingerprints under a versioned
vocabulary.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Shape claim, restated]
$\text{ShapeClaim} = (\text{shape},\, \text{conditioning},\, \text{role},\,
\text{epistemic state})$; four orthogonal axes, never collapsed into a scalar.
Conditioning $\in$ \{failure, success, mixed, frontier\}. Role $\in$ \{regularity,
obstruction, enabling\_condition, boundary\_hypothesis\}. Epistemic state $\in$
\{proposed, challenged, surviving, weakened, split, falsified, operator\_attested\}.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Geometry, restated]
$\mathcal{G}(W) = (R, B, H, V, T)$: regimes, boundaries, shape claims, voids,
trajectories.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Boundary delta]
$\delta(b)$: minimal structural condition separating a counterexample (or
observed split) from a claim's support population; produced during adversarial
challenge; itself a first-class record.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Structural delta]
$\Delta S$: a proposed change in structural shape crossing a boundary. Must
state at least one non-cosmetic field change and identify its nearest failure
family.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Projection]
$\Pi_{\text{domain}}: \Delta S \mapsto W'$: model-assisted translation of a
structural delta into a domain-native candidate.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Landing point]
$\Lambda(W') = (O(W'),\, E(W'),\, \text{strength})$: observed result of
verifying $W'$ in the domain, with the verifier tier recorded.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Operators.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $\text{normalize}: \text{raw work} \to S$ (model-assisted, vocabulary-resolved)
  \item $\text{cluster}: \{S_i\} \to \{R_j\}$ (deterministic under signature + regime)
  \item $\text{compress}: R_j \to H$ (model-native shape claim generation)
  \item $\text{challenge}: H \to (H',\, \delta)$ (seven probes; disposition
    $\in$ \{survive, weaken, split, falsify\})
  \item $\text{project}: (H, \delta) \to \Delta S$ (structural move against a
    surviving boundary)
  \item $\text{compile}: \Delta S \to W'$ (domain projection)
  \item $\text{verify}: W' \to \Lambda(W')$ (strongest decisive verifier first)
  \item $\text{update}: (\mathcal{G}, W', \Lambda) \to \mathcal{G}'$ (re-entry
    plus search-policy mutation)
\end{itemize}

\section{Falsification Conditions}
\label{app:falsification}

The GoW thesis is refereeable only if it states what future evidence would
make it uninteresting or wrong. Each of the five conditions below is a target
for adversarial evaluation: a demonstration of any one would substantively
weaken the framework's claim on the literature.

\paragraph{F1. Geometry is vacuous.}
If the structural descriptions assigned to work items are so model-dependent
that different reasonable description procedures produce incommensurable
geometries, then the ``geometry'' is a projection artifact rather than a
property of the work. Test: run structural normalization with two independent
model families on the same corpus; measure agreement on regime membership and
boundary identification. \emph{Threshold:} pairwise regime-membership
agreement (e.g., Cohen's $\kappa$ or exact-match rate on a predeclared regime
assignment) below a level considered practically usable for the intended
application --- a threshold to be fixed in the preregistration for the run
that performs this test, not asserted here in the absence of that data ---
counts as F1 holding.

\paragraph{F2. Shape-space search is dominated.}
If undirected generation (without shape-space reasoning) consistently produces
equal or better proposals measured by structural proximity to withheld advances,
then the shape-space detour adds cost without information. Test: multi-trial
head-to-head between undirected generation and shape-guided generation over a
benchmark of held-out targets, with predeclared endpoint. \emph{Threshold:}
undirected generation matching or exceeding shape-guided generation's recovery
rate at a predeclared one-sided test (e.g., exact binomial or McNemar, as in
\cref{sec:superiority}) with significance level $\alpha$ fixed in the
preregistration, over a predeclared minimum number of held-out targets, counts
as F2 holding for that benchmark.

\paragraph{F3. Epistemic overhead without epistemic gain.}
If the challenge lifecycle and provenance machinery produce no measurably
different outcomes from unstructured ``try again with the failures in context,''
then the epistemics are ceremony rather than discipline. Test: compare
GoW-with-challenge against a challenge-free ablation on the same corpus and
target; measure proposal quality, redundancy, and false-positive rate for
structural claims. \emph{Threshold:} no predeclared effect-size difference
(e.g., no reduction in redundant-proposal rate or false-positive structural
claims beyond a minimum effect size fixed in the preregistration) between the
two arms across a predeclared minimum number of trials counts as F3 holding.

\paragraph{F4. Domain projection is the bottleneck.}
If model-generated structural deltas cannot be reliably compiled into
domain-verifiable candidates (projection failure is the norm rather than the
exception), then shape-space reasoning is systematically disconnected from
domain-space truth. Test: measure the rate at which structural deltas produce
candidates that (a) parse under the domain's verifier and (b) are
mechanistically distinct from the failure population, across multiple domains.
\emph{Threshold:} a projection-success rate (candidates satisfying both (a)
and (b)) below a predeclared minimum rate, averaged across a predeclared
minimum number of domains and trials, counts as F4 holding.

\paragraph{F5. The method does not transfer.}
If GoW only produces interesting observations on problems whose failure history
was curated by the GoW practitioner, then it is a curation framework rather than
a search method. Test: apply GoW to at least one domain not authored by the
research team, using an existing external corpus of prior work.
\emph{Threshold:} on that external corpus, a rate of admitted candidate
invariants or surviving shape claims not distinguishably above the rate
expected under a predeclared null (e.g., a random or naive-grouping baseline
over the same corpus) counts as F5 holding.

\paragraph{Aggregate falsification condition.}
The conjunction of F1--F5 is the strongest test. GoW should be considered
uninteresting as a general framework if all five conditions hold; it should be
considered wrong as a specific method if F1 or F4 hold in isolation. None of
F1--F5 has been run against a predeclared threshold as of this manuscript;
the thresholds above describe the decision rule a future run must fix in
advance, not a result already obtained.
