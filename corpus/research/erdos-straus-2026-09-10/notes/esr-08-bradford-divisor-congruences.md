## Source-supported account

Bradford describes a two-variable search using a smallest denominator x and a divisor d of x². The paper states correspondences between its modular conditions and the original solution triples. Existence of a suitable pair for every prime is stated as a conjecture, separately from the correspondence claims.

## Boundary and curator interpretation

A bijection can be correct even when its domain is empty for a particular prime. Preserve the distinction between a solution-set reformulation and an existence theorem. Ordering conventions, divisor bounds, and congruences must travel with a reconstructed witness.

## Mechanistic relationship

Related to factorization and Type I/II work (train/es-03, es-04). Adds a specific searchable reduction, not proof that all earlier representations were structurally wrong.

## Grounding task

Check both mapping directions on finite examples. Failure to find a pair within a restricted search window is not a counterexample to the unrestricted conjecture.
